# Source critique: *Hallucination as Context Drift*

**Source reviewed:** [arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.21666v1), with the [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21666) and the saved raw HTML at `sources/context-drift-2606.21666v1.html`. The raw HTML is load-bearing here because clean extraction omitted Algorithm 1. This is a critique of what the paper reports, not an admission to run an Antelligence experiment.

## Bottom line

The paper supports a narrow result: in its induced three-agent travel-planning setup, **full-context broadcast was worse than no synchronization on the paper's assertion-level HR**, while SSVP's small point estimate over no-sync was not statistically significant. It does **not** establish that SSVP is a verified communication policy, that CDS detects hallucination, that graph topology was studied, or that the result transfers to Antelligence swarms.

The headline post is materially too smooth: the paper uses one Claude model family, unbalanced travel sample sizes (`30/30/15`, not `30/30/30`), no reported assertion denominators, and no matched token/cost budget. The software replication is small and does not reproduce the contamination effect. Also, raw Algorithm 1 shows that SSVP first broadcasts summaries to **all peers**, gates on the **system mean CDS**, and only then sends full context between high-drift pairs; it is not a pair-only threshold policy.

## Is hallucination scored independently of agreement?

**Mostly yes, as defined—but this is not an independent correctness audit.** In §4.4 the paper defines HR as the fraction of **agent assertions** inconsistent with initialization ground truth and says it samples “up to 8 agent outputs and evaluate[s] each independently.” That makes HR a factuality metric rather than an agreement/consensus metric. Agreement is discussed separately through TCS (final-plan coherence) and the qualitative examples in §5.7.

Important qualifications:

- The paper does not report the exact number of assertions, the numerator/denominator by condition, assertion extraction rules, or whether repeated copies of one wrong belief are deduplicated. If repeated assertions count separately—as the wording suggests—broadcast can increase the weight of one contaminated belief without measuring unique factual failures.
- “Structured facts account for 78%” and an “independent LLM judge” evaluates the remaining 22% of free-text claims (§4.4). The paper does not specify the structured-fact checker, HR judge model/version, prompt, blinding to condition, access to full transcripts/other agents, or adjudication rules.
- Two judge calls on only 20 sampled outputs produced `κ=0.79` (§4.4). This is inter-rater consistency, not agent confidence, calibration, truth independence, or evidence that the metric is independent of the treatment.
- TCS is a separate 0–1 LLM-judged score over date, location, budget, schedule, and recommendation accuracy (§4.4). The paper explicitly says source attribution and confidence scoring remain future work (§6.2).

## Denominators, samples, pairing, and budgets

- **HR unit:** assertion-level fraction, with “up to 8 agent outputs per trial” (§4.4). The paper reports no exact assertion counts or per-condition denominators. The displayed values in Table 1 are means with a column labeled `HR (95%CI)`, but it does not say whether `±` is a CI half-width, standard error, or another summary.
- **Run counts:** Table 1 footnote and Table 3 specify travel `n=30` no-sync, `n=30` SSVP, and `n=15` full-broadcast; total travel `n=75`. This conflicts with the abstract's “n=30 per condition” and §5.1's “30 trials per main condition.” Software is `10/10/5` in Table 4. The conclusion's “100 total trials” is consistent with `75+25`, but not with 30 per condition for all travel rows.
- **Paired structure:** The paper says destinations are sampled uniformly per trial but does not say whether conditions share the same scenario draws, random seeds, or matched trial IDs. It reports Welch t-tests and Cohen's *d* (Table 3), with no paired analysis, blocking, cluster treatment, or direction convention for the displayed `Δ`, *t*, and *d* signs.
- **Reported HR:** Table 1 gives no-sync `0.492 ± 0.039`, SSVP `0.463 ± 0.031`, full-broadcast `0.658 ± 0.084`. Table 3 gives no-sync vs SSVP `p=0.257, d=0.30`; no-sync vs full-broadcast `p=0.0022, d=1.18`; SSVP vs full-broadcast `p=0.0005, d=1.47`. Thus the SSVP-vs-no-sync reduction is a non-significant point estimate, not established improvement.
- **Significance-label inconsistency:** Table 1 marks full-broadcast `***` with a footnote `p<0.01`; Table 3 uses `***` for `p<0.001`; Table 4 also says `***p<0.001` for travel. The numeric p-values are present, but the legend is not consistent.
- **Budget:** Table 2 reports API calls/trial: no-sync `18`, SSVP `53` total (`17.6` sync), full-broadcast `126` total (`54.0` sync). The paper reports SSVP as 58% fewer calls than full-broadcast, but **calls are not tokens, latency, or money**. No input/output token counts, prompt lengths, retries, parallelism, rate limits, embedding calls, or failure handling are reported. Full-broadcast has half the travel runs because it costs approximately seven times as much per trial (Table 1 footnote), so the comparison is not a matched resource envelope.
- **Treatment confounding:** Full-broadcast changes payload size, context exposure, timing, number of calls, and likely context-window pressure. SSVP changes summary length, all-peer summary dissemination, global-mean gating, conditional pair exchange, merge prompting, explicit contradiction reasoning, and extra inference. The result cannot isolate “topology” from message content, verification prompt, or added reasoning.

## Topology selection is not topology/routing research

**No, the paper did not vary topology or routing as an experimental factor.** Raw Algorithm 1 specifies one fixed communication pattern: (i) summaries broadcast to **all peers** at a sync event (lines 546–560); (ii) pairwise CDS is computed for every pair (lines 563–575); (iii) a **system-level mean** gate `CDS_sys(t)>τ` controls whether (iv) only high-drift pairs exchange full context and reconcile (lines 583–615). The paper does not report adjacency matrices, graph alternatives, edge budgets, ring/star/fully-connected variants, random-routing controls, or learned routers.

So SSVP's pair exchange is not evidence for a pair-only or locally gated graph policy. It is a conditional second stage after an all-pairs measurement and fleet-average trigger. Nor does the algorithm support a claim that summaries fully replace state: line 14 invokes `ContextMerge`, line 15 re-embeds the merged context, and §3.3 says agents do not silently overwrite state.

The result is therefore not evidence that a chosen graph is better. It is evidence—under this setup—that **one particular two-stage prompt-and-payload policy** behaved differently from two broad communication regimes. SSVP's “verification” remains LLM adjudication, with no external source oracle.

## Main confounds and extrapolation errors

1. **Injected error geometry is doing much of the work.** Full-broadcast copies Booking's wrong destination to other agents in the stated qualitative mechanism (§5.1, §5.7). This demonstrates vulnerability to a deliberately cascading bad belief, not a general law that more communication is harmful.
2. **The “verified summary” claim is too strong.** SSVP surfaces contradictions and asks an LLM to adjudicate; it does not independently verify truth, provenance, or source identity. Its zero erroneous-state adoptions in 30 travel trials is explicitly attributed to unambiguous mismatches (§6.2).
3. **Truth and agreement can move together in the wrong direction.** Full-broadcast has the lowest CDS (`0.097–0.107` from step 2) but the highest HR (§5.3), illustrating that consensus can be confidently wrong. This is a useful warning, not proof that CDS is predictive: no-sync max CDS versus HR is reported as `r=-0.03`.
4. **Domain extrapolation is not supported.** Travel is semantically cascading; software is lower-HR and apparently more orthogonal (§5.8). The contamination effect does not replicate: software HR is `0.188/0.150/0.150` (Table 4). No evidence supports healthcare, legal, financial, open-ended, dynamic, or physical-swarm deployment.
5. **Statistical precision is overstated by the headline.** Full-broadcast has `n=15`, no exact assertion denominators, apparently unpaired Welch tests, and an unbalanced cost-driven sample. There is no correction or preregistered analysis plan described, and no software-domain p-values are supplied.
6. **No credible-conflict or adversarial-source test.** The paper itself flags the likely weak point: two equally authoritative but conflicting API results (§6.2). That is precisely where a source-aware verifier, confidence, provenance, or Antelligence memory policy would need to earn its keep.
7. **Release/reproduction gap.** The retrieved arXiv record has a “Code, Data and Media Associated with this Article” heading but no repository entry. §4.2 says the full implementation, prompts, scenarios, and rubrics “will be released upon publication.” I found no exact-title first-party code link to inspect; this is a scoped retrieval result, not a claim that no code exists anywhere.

## Antelligence relevance: testable hypotheses, not copied conclusions

The paper is relevant as a **failure-mode warning**: a shared trail can spread a bad belief faster than it spreads truth. It does not validate a Queen, pheromone memory, learned routing, or graph novelty for Antelligence.

1. **Agreement is not correctness.** In a seeded bad-node condition, test whether agreement rises while independent ground-truth error also rises. Score assertion truth, unique-fact truth, cross-agent agreement, and final task utility separately.
2. **Fleet gate versus pair gate.** Hold content and budget fixed while comparing the paper's system-mean gate with a per-pair gate. Test whether a single high-divergence pair should trigger local reconciliation or fleet-wide action.
3. **All-peer summaries versus selective summaries.** Compare the Algorithm 1 all-peer summary broadcast with pair-only summaries, fixed ring/star dissemination, and random same-budget edges. Keep summary length, timing, and full-context exchange rules constant.
4. **Verification must be external to the claimant.** Compare raw pheromone/summaries, source-tagged summaries, deterministic schema/provenance checks, and an independent verifier that cannot rewrite the evidence. Include equally credible conflicting sources, not only an obviously wrong airport code.
5. **Use the controls the paper lacks.** Preserve independent first attempts, solo, vote, no-memory, shuffled-memory, stale-memory, and same-envelope random-routing controls. Treat abstention and invalid output as assigned-case outcomes, not silently dropped cases.
6. **Budget-match before claiming efficiency.** Record input/output tokens, calls, retries, latency, and dollar cost. Compare quality at equal resource envelopes; “58% fewer calls” alone is not a cost or efficiency result.
7. **Pre-register the estimand and pairing.** Reuse the same scenario/seed across conditions where appropriate, report exact assertion denominators and confidence intervals, and use paired analysis when the design is paired. Test whether CDS predicts future independent truth failures, not merely whether it triggers the policy.
8. **Test task geometry.** Vary cascading shared facts versus orthogonal role facts, error severity, source freshness, confidence, and number of agents. The paper's own travel/software split predicts an interaction, not a universal synchronization win.
